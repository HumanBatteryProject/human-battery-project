## The strategic point first

Your instinct to separate three levels is right, and there's a sharper version of it available.

**The microtubule biophysics and Orch OR are separable claims, and you should separate them deliberately.**

Orch OR bundles a physics claim (Penrose's Objective Reduction), a biology claim (microtubules host quantum processes), and a philosophy claim (this explains consciousness). Most of the scientific resistance is to the third. If you import the bundle, you inherit the resistance to consciousness claims you don't need and can't defend.

The middle claim, that microtubules do organized electrical, vibrational and optical work, has its own literature, its own experiments, and its own clinical anchor in anesthesia. That's the part relevant to a program about biological energy. Take it. Leave consciousness to Penrose.

## Level 1: Demonstrated

**Microtubules are electrically polar, vibrating structures.** Tubulin dimers carry large permanent dipole moments, arranged in a helical lattice. The mechanical and electrical modes are real and measurable. The definitive honest framing comes from the title of a review by Kučera, Havelka & Cifra (*Wave Motion*, 2016): **"Vibrations of microtubules: Physics that has not met biology yet."** That is the state of the field in six words, from people working in it.

Pokorný's contribution, microtubules as electrically polar oscillators with GTP hydrolysis as a candidate energy source, is a serious theoretical proposal with experimental support in cancer-cell dielectric measurements (Pokorný, Vedruccio & Kučera, *European Biophysics Journal* 2011). Tuszynski's group has published extensively on ionic wave propagation and memristor-like current-voltage behaviour in microtubules.

**Anesthetics bind microtubules.** This is the strongest and most clinically grounded piece of the whole pillar. Rod Eckenhoff's lab at Penn systematically studied anesthetic binding across neuronal proteins using genomic, proteomic and optogenetic methods, and found volatile anesthetics exert functional effects on cytoskeletal microtubules. Independently supported by a clinical observation: (cite index="152-1">taxane chemotherapy consisting of microtubule-stabilizing drugs reduces the effectiveness of anesthesia during surgery in human cancer patients.</cite> Microtubule disruption is also implicated in post-operative cognitive dysfunction.

None of that requires quantum mechanics. It's pharmacology.

**Microtubules propagate optical excitations, and anesthetics damp them.** Kalra et al., *ACS Central Science* 2023, the paper co-authored across Craddock, Hameroff, Tuszynski, Penrose and Scholes. (cite index="152-1">Tryptophan fluorescence lifetimes propagated and persisted due to quantum exciton mechanisms, and were dampened by two types of anesthetic, isoflurane and etomidate.</cite> As noted in Addendum III, the same group found energy diffusing roughly five times further than classical calculation predicts.

**This is where the tryptophan thread closes.** Kurian's superradiance is tryptophan. Kalra and Scholes' microtubule exciton migration is tryptophan fluorescence. Two independent lines, two different labs, same amino acid, same structures. That convergence is the strongest thing in this entire pillar and it's the piece that connects to your program, because tryptophan is dietary.

## Level 2: Experimental evidence, unsettled

**The anesthesia prediction was made in advance and it held.** Khan, Huang, Timuçin et al. (Wiest lab, Wellesley), "Microtubule-Stabilizer Epothilone B Delays Anesthetic-Induced Unconsciousness in Rats," *eNeuro* 2024;11(8):ENEURO.0291-24.2024 (PMID 39147581). Rats given a microtubule-stabilizing drug took measurably longer to lose the righting reflex under 4% isoflurane. A follow-up in mice was published in *Neuropharmacology* in 2026.

The theory made a prospective, falsifiable, non-obvious prediction and the experiment confirmed it. That is how science is supposed to work, and it is unusual in this field. Worth noting the provenance: the work sat within the Templeton World Charity Foundation's Accelerating Research in Consciousness program, and Wiest's own characterization is that Orch OR (cite index="152-1">produced the only confirmatory evidence among theories tested.</cite> That's a proponent's summary of a program he participated in, accurate as far as it goes, but not a neutral verdict.

**Delayed luminescence, the finding most relevant to you.** Zoghi, Batarseh, Wu, Tuszynski & Dogariu (2025): blue-light pulses on microtubules produce (cite index="153-1">"light-trapping" and re-emission over hundreds of milliseconds, over a second for full microtubules. Crucially, anesthetics shorten this delayed luminescence time, while structurally similar non-anesthetics do not.</cite>

Read that carefully against Addendum III. I told you the timescale gap was the fatal problem, quantum effects at picoseconds, your interventions at days. This is **hundreds of milliseconds to over a second.** That's twelve orders of magnitude slower than superradiance and lands in the range where biology actually operates. A protein structure that absorbs light and releases it over a second is behaving like a capacitor.

The anesthetic/non-anesthetic control is what makes it credible. A structurally similar molecule that doesn't cause unconsciousness also doesn't shorten the luminescence. That's a real control, not a correlation.

**Bandyopadhyay's resonances, treat with caution.** His group reports (cite index="167-1">self-similar resonances in microtubules repeating in terahertz, gigahertz, megahertz and kilohertz frequency ranges</cite>, with AC conductance through single microtubules at those resonant frequencies. If it holds it's remarkable. But independent replication is thin, the work sits largely in specialist venues, and it has been enthusiastically adopted by people making claims Bandyopadhyay himself doesn't make, including the leap from "microtubules resonate in the GHz range" to "wireless devices therefore affect every life form on the planet." That inference appears in material citing his work and it is not established. **Cite the primary papers if you cite this at all, never the downstream interpretation.**

## Level 3: Theoretical / speculative

**Orch OR itself.** Penrose supplies Objective Reduction, gravitational self-energy causing wavefunction collapse, non-computable, invoked to explain non-algorithmic human understanding. Hameroff supplies microtubules as substrate. As a theory of consciousness it is not established and is a minority position in neuroscience.

**The Tegmark exchange, know both sides precisely.** Tegmark (*Phys Rev E*, 2000) calculated microtubule decoherence at ~10⁻¹³ seconds, roughly ten orders of magnitude too fast for anything functionally relevant. Hagan, Hameroff & Tuszynski (*Phys Rev E* 2002;65:061901) responded that (cite index="157-1">Tegmark erred, using a superposition separation distance of tens of nanometres rather than femtometres as stipulated in Orch OR</cite>, recalculating coherence at 10⁻⁵-10⁻⁴ seconds.

Two things to hold at once. The rebuttal identifies a genuine technical disagreement about a specific parameter, and it was published in the same journal. And the Orch OR camp's own review concedes the outcome: (cite index="157-1">"Nevertheless, Tegmark's paper supported widespread skepticism that macroscopic or mesoscopic quantum effects could persist long enough at biological temperatures to be functionally relevant."</cite>

Note also that the words "Tegmark erred" come from a Hameroff-aligned paper. Present it as a disputed parameter with a published exchange, not as a settled correction.

**Further out still:** Kerskens & Perez (2022) claimed MRI evidence of entanglement-witness signals in living human brains correlated with consciousness and working memory, highly contested. Mavromatos et al. (2025) propose microtubules as high-Q QED cavities, purely theoretical. Both belong in a footnote at most.

## What it does earn you

**A third convergence on tryptophan.** Kurian's superradiance, Kalra and Scholes' exciton migration, and the delayed-luminescence work all centre on the same aromatic amino acid in the same structures. That's not proof of anything, but three independent lines pointing at one dietary molecule is the kind of thing worth writing down and watching. The kynurenine:tryptophan ratio I proposed in Addendum III now has a second reason to be on the panel.

**A defensible non-consciousness claim.** "Microtubules are optically and electrically active structures, anesthetics act on them, and this is measurable" is supportable without touching Orch OR. That's the version to put in the book.

**A demonstration of how to handle contested science.** The Wiest experiment is the best example available of doing this right, a prospective prediction, a real control group, a falsifiable outcome, published in a mainstream journal. If cohort 01 is designed with that same discipline, you are operating at the standard of the people you're citing rather than the standard of the people quoting them.

1. **Kučera, Havelka & Cifra, "Vibrations of microtubules: Physics that has not met biology yet"** (*Wave Motion* 2016). Start here. It is the honest baseline from working physicists.
2. **Kalra et al.** (*ACS Central Science* 2023). The tryptophan exciton work. Most relevant to your model.

4. **Tegmark 2000 and Hagan, Hameroff & Tuszynski 2002, together.** Read the exchange, not either side alone.
5. **Hameroff & Penrose** review of Orch OR. Read last, knowing everything above.
6. **Pokorný and Tuszynski** for depth on the electrical and vibrational side.

Bandyopadhyay only in primary sources, and only if you're citing the resonance claims specifically.
