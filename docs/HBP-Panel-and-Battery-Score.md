# The Panel and the Battery Score

*What gets drawn, why each marker is there, and exactly how the number is calculated.*

---

## Part one: the panel

Thirty markers, drawn twice: within the week before day 1, and within the week before day 90. Same lab, same fasting state, same time of morning both times. That last part matters more than people expect, cortisol and testosterone both move substantially across the morning, and a 7am draw compared against a 11am draw produces a change that isn't real.

Markers are grouped into the four subsystems. The grouping is the product: a client gets four readings they can hold in their head, not thirty numbers they can't.

### Charge: how well you handle fuel

| Marker | Unit | Lab range | Program-optimal | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Glucose, fasting | mg/dL | 70-99 | 75-90 | The baseline. Cheap, universal, and moves within weeks. |
| Insulin, fasting | µIU/mL | 2-19.6 | 2-6 | Rises years before glucose does. This is the marker that catches people whose "normal bloodwork" is hiding the problem. |
| HbA1c | % | 4.0-5.6 | 4.6-5.3 | Ninety-day average. It exists to corroborate, since the draw interval matches the red cell lifespan almost exactly. |
| HOMA-IR | index |, | 0.5-1.5 | Calculated from glucose × insulin. The single best cheap read on insulin resistance. |
| Triglycerides | mg/dL | <150 | 40-90 | Highly responsive to carbohydrate load and alcohol. Moves fast. |
| Triglyceride : HDL ratio | ratio | <3.0 | 0.5-1.5 | Calculated. Tracks metabolic health better than either component alone. |

### Drain: what is quietly consuming capacity

| Marker | Unit | Lab range | Program-optimal | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hs-CRP | mg/L | <3.0 | 0-0.8 | The anchor inflammatory marker. Sensitive to acute illness, so a client with a cold reschedules the draw. |
| GGT | U/L | 0-65 | 5-20 | Liver enzyme, strongly responsive to alcohol and metabolic load. Also a decent oxidative-stress proxy. |
| ALT | U/L | 0-55 | 10-25 | Elevated ALT in a non-drinker is usually fatty liver, which is a metabolic finding. |
| AST | U/L | 0-48 | 10-25 | Paired with ALT. The ratio distinguishes patterns. |
| Uric acid | mg/dL | 3.5-7.2 | 3.5-5.5 | Tracks fructose intake and metabolic stress. Underused. |
| White blood cell count | K/µL | 3.4-10.8 | 4.5-6.5 | Scored as a band. High and low both mean something. |
| Homocysteine | µmol/L | <15 | 5-8 | Methylation status. Responds to B12, folate and B6. |

### Output: what you can actually spend

| Marker | Unit | Lab range | Program-optimal | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TSH | µIU/mL | 0.45-4.5 | 0.8-2.0 | Scored as a band, penalised both directions. |
| Free T3 | pg/mL | 2.0-4.4 | 3.0-4.2 | The active hormone. Drops under caloric restriction, which is exactly why it's measured on a program that restricts. |
| Free T4 | ng/dL | 0.82-1.77 | 1.0-1.5 | Substrate for T3. The T4-to-T3 conversion is the interesting part. |
| Testosterone, total | ng/dL | 264-916 | 500-900 | Male reference range. Female clients use a sex-specific band. |
| Testosterone, free | pg/mL | 6.6-18.1 | 12-25 | What's actually available. Total can look fine while free is low. |
| DHEA-S | µg/dL | 70-495 | 200-450 | Adrenal reserve. Declines with age and with chronic load. |
| Cortisol, morning | µg/dL | 6.2-19.4 | 10-16 | Band-scored. **Timing-critical**, must be drawn within an hour of the same clock time both times. |
| IGF-1 | ng/mL | 50-300 | 120-200 | Band-scored. Both ends carry risk. |
| SHBG | nmol/L | 10-50 | 20-45 | Needed to interpret testosterone. Also moves with insulin. |

### Reserve: the raw material the other three draw on

| Marker | Unit | Lab range | Program-optimal | Why it's here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 25-OH Vitamin D | ng/mL | 30-100 | 40-70 | The direct readout on the light protocol. Also the marker most often deficient at intake. |
| Ferritin | ng/mL | 30-400 | 50-150 | Iron storage, but also an acute-phase reactant, read alongside hs-CRP or it misleads. |
| Iron saturation | % | 15-55 | 25-40 | Distinguishes true iron status from inflammation-driven ferritin. |
| Vitamin B12 | pg/mL | 232-1245 | 500-900 | The lower half of the reference range is where symptomatic people live. |
| Folate, serum | ng/mL | 3.0-20.0 | 10-20 | Pairs with B12 and homocysteine. |
| Magnesium, RBC | mg/dL | 4.0-6.4 | 5.5-6.4 | **Red cell**, not serum. Serum magnesium is tightly regulated and tells you almost nothing. |
| Albumin | g/dL | 3.9-4.9 | 4.3-4.9 | Protein status and a general vitality marker. |
| HDL cholesterol | mg/dL | >39 | 55-90 | Sits in Reserve rather than Charge because it functions as substrate capacity here. |

### Notes on ordering

Roughly twenty of these come from a standard comprehensive metabolic panel, CBC, lipid panel, and thyroid panel, routine and inexpensive. The cost sits in the additions: fasting insulin, hs-CRP, homocysteine, free T3, free testosterone, DHEA-S, IGF-1, SHBG, RBC magnesium, and vitamin D.

Two are calculated rather than drawn, HOMA-IR and the triglyceride:HDL ratio, so they cost nothing.

**Draw conditions, both times:** twelve-hour fast, water only, no alcohol for 48 hours, no training for 24 hours, and the same hour of the morning. Reschedule if the client is acutely ill, since hs-CRP and ferritin will both be wrong.

## Part two: how it's scored

### Each marker gets 0-100 based on the program-optimal band, not the lab range

This is the core decision. The lab's reference range is a population distribution. It tells you where 95% of people fall, and it includes a lot of people who feel exactly like your clients feel. Scoring against it would give most of your cohort full marks on day 1 and leave nothing to improve.

So the scale is anchored on the optimal band instead:

- **100**: inside the program-optimal band
- **50**: at the edge of the lab reference range
- **0**: at twice that distance from optimal

Linear between those points, clamped at both ends.

Worked example, HbA1c. Optimal 4.6-5.3, reference ceiling 5.6.

| Value | Score | Reading |
|---|---|---|
| 5.0 | 100 | In band |
| 5.3 | 100 | Top of band |
| 5.4 | 83 | Drifting |
| 5.6 | 50 | At the reference edge: a doctor would call this normal |
| 5.9 | 0 | |

A value of 5.6 scoring 50 while a physician calls it normal is the whole thesis in one row.

Markers with a band on both sides, TSH, cortisol, IGF-1, white cell count, are penalised in both directions. TSH at 0.45 and TSH at 4.5 both score 50.

Markers where only one direction matters use one bound. Vitamin D scores 100 anywhere from 40 to 70, and drops on both sides, because too much is also a finding.

### Subsystem scores

Each subsystem is the plain average of its available marker scores.

**Missing markers are excluded, never imputed.** If a lab didn't run free T3, it doesn't quietly become an average score. It isn't counted. This is the difference between a score that reflects the panel and one that flatters it.

**A subsystem needs at least two markers to produce a score.** One marker is a data point, not a system reading.

### The composite

Weighted average of whatever subsystems scored:

| Subsystem | Weight |
|---|---|
| Charge | 0.30 |
| Drain | 0.25 |
| Output | 0.25 |
| Reserve | 0.20 |

Charge carries the most weight because it's the most responsive to the protocol and the most predictive of long-term outcome. Reserve carries the least because it's substrate rather than function.

Weights are renormalised over the subsystems actually present, so a partial panel still yields a comparable number. A charge-only interim draw produces a valid composite from that subsystem alone.

Both the weights and the version are stored, so a score computed in 2026 is still interpretable in 2031 when the methodology has moved on.

### What it produces

A verified run against a realistic panel: a metabolically struggling 45-year-old at day 0, meaningfully improved at day 90:

| | Day 0 | Day 90 | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Composite** | **54.8** | **99.1** | **+44.2** |
| Charge | 23.9 | 98.3 | +74.4 |
| Drain | 65.8 | 98.4 | +32.6 |
| Output | 78.0 | 100.0 | +22.0 |
| Reserve | 58.6 | 100.0 | +41.4 |

The system also ranks the worst markers, which is what the coaching targets first. For that client: HOMA-IR at 0, triglyceride:HDL at 3, vitamin D at 5, HbA1c at 17, triglycerides at 20. Four of the five are Charge, which is why phase one removes sugar and alcohol before it adds anything.

Every score stores its per-marker breakdown, so a client's number can be explained line by line rather than asserted.

## Three things that need your decision

**The optimal bands are mine, not yours.** I seeded them from functional-medicine convention and they're defensible, but they're the single most consequential set of numbers in the program. They define what "good" means for every client. They should be reviewed and signed off before cohort 01, and the reasoning documented, because someone will eventually ask why 5.3 and not 5.4.

**Sex-specific ranges aren't built yet.** Testosterone, free testosterone, DHEA-S and ferritin all need different bands for female clients. Right now the seeded values are male. This needs fixing before you enroll women, and the schema supports it. It's an extra column and a lookup, not a redesign.

**The scale may be generous at the top.** In the test above, day 90 landed at 99. If most clients saturate near 100, the score stops discriminating between good and excellent. Worth watching across cohort 01 and tightening the optimal bands in a v2 if it happens.

---

## The standing constraint

The Battery Score is a composite index built for this program. It is not diagnostic. It is not validated for clinical use. It must never be presented to a clinician as a medical measurement, and no individual marker may be interpreted as evidence of a condition.

Any result outside the lab's reference range gets referred to a physician under the written referral protocol, and that referral is recorded against the panel.
