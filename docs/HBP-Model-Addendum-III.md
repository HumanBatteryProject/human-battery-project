## The correction that has to come first

Quantum biology's founding result has been substantially walked back, and you need to know this before you cite anyone in this field.

Gregory Engel's 2007 *Nature* paper on the Fenna-Matthews-Olson complex, long-lived oscillations in a photosynthetic protein, interpreted as electronic quantum coherence, launched the entire field. It is the story every popular account of quantum biology opens with.

It did not hold in its original form. Duan et al. (*PNAS* 2017), from the Miller, Thorwart and Cogdell groups, found <cite index="125-1">no low-frequency quantum beats with dephasing times beyond 60 femtoseconds</cite> at biologically relevant temperatures. The subsequent review, Cao et al., "Quantum biology revisited," *Science Advances* 2020, states the position plainly: <cite index="125-1">several recent studies of this complex each reach the same conclusion, that the observed long-lived quantum beats are inconsistent with interexciton coherence; instead these oscillations mostly show characteristics of Raman-active vibrational modes on the electronic ground-state surface, unrelated to the energy transfer process.</cite>

The field's current position is **vibronic**, not purely electronic: <cite index="126-1">many experimentally observed long-lived oscillatory signatures originate predominantly from excitonic-vibrational interactions and environmentally modulated vibronic dynamics rather than sustained purely electronic coherence.</cite>

And it is still live. A 2025 *Science Advances* paper argues the debunking went too far. That <cite index="127-1">the oversimplified noise model in Duan et al. substantially underestimates the lifetime of excitonic coherences, and their measures do not provide conclusive evidence against quantum effects in FMO.</cite>

**Why this matters to you.** If you cite "quantum coherence in photosynthesis proves biology is quantum," you're citing a 2007 interpretation that the field spent fifteen years qualifying. The defensible version: photosynthetic energy transfer involves genuine ultrafast quantum dynamics with mixed electronic-vibrational character, and whether long-lived coherence plays a functional role remains actively contested.

This is also the single best calibration available for the whole field. The founding result got softer, not harder, under scrutiny. Assume the same about everything downstream.

## Philip Kurian: the most relevant work on this list

You were right to flag him. Kurian's group is doing the only work here that touches your model directly, and it's published in real venues.

**The finding.** Babcock, Montes-Cabrera, Oberhofer, Chergui, Celardo & Kurian, "Ultraviolet superradiance from mega-networks of tryptophan in biological architectures," *J Phys Chem B* 2024;128(17):4035-4046. Large tryptophan networks in cellular structures <cite index="115-1">display robust cooperative effects suggesting the formation of superradiant states in a thermal environment, with the specific architectures of these networks, found in transmembrane proteins, cytoskeletal filaments, sub-neuronal elements, photoreceptor complexes and virion capsids, playing an essential role in stimulating this superradiance.</cite>

**Why it matters for the Human Battery specifically.** The proposed function is *photoprotection against your own metabolism*:

<cite index="114-1">To break down food, cells undergoing aerobic respiration use oxygen and generate free radicals, which can emit damaging, high-energy UV light particles. Tryptophan can absorb this ultraviolet light and re-emit it at a lower energy. And, as the study found, very large tryptophan networks can do this even more efficiently and robustly because of their powerful quantum effects.</cite>

Read that against your four subsystems. Aerobic respiration is Charge. Free radicals are Drain. Tryptophan networks sit between them as a quantum-optical damping layer. Kurian's own framing: <cite index="118-1">"This photoprotection may prove crucial in ameliorating or halting the progression of degenerative illness."</cite>

That is the first mechanism anyone in this literature has offered that connects two of your measured subsystems through a single physical process.

**Independent convergence.** Kalra & Scholes (2023) tracked energy propagating through brain microtubules with a fluorescent dye and found <cite index="121-1">energy diffused about five times further than expected according to classical calculations, suggesting a quantum phenomenon was at play. "It's likely some kind of quantum resonance," says Scholes.</cite> Different lab, different method, same structures. And Michael Levin, from the bioelectricity layer in Addendum II, commented on Kurian's work: <cite index="118-1">"Such quantum optical networks are widespread, not only in neural systems but broadly throughout the web of life."</cite>

**The honest caveat.** Superradiance here is measured in **picoseconds**. <cite index="119-1">Superradiance in these cytoskeletal filaments happens in about a picosecond, a millionth of a microsecond.</cite> The claim that this constitutes cellular information processing "billions of times faster than chemical processes" is Kurian's extrapolation, not a measured biological function. The spectroscopic signature is real. What the cell *does* with it is hypothesis.

## The radical pair mechanism: where magnetism becomes legitimate

This is the part that partially rehabilitates something you've been reading, but in a much narrower and more specific form than Kruse presents it.

**Ritz** established the framework: cryptochrome-based photoreceptor magnetoreception in birds (Ritz, Adem & Schulten, *Biophysical Journal* 2000). Light hits cryptochrome, creates a radical pair, and the pair's electron spins evolve differently depending on the ambient magnetic field, changing the reaction's product yield. That's a genuine mechanism by which a magnetic field far weaker than thermal energy can bias chemistry.

**Aiello** extended it in a way that matters. Her group's 2024 *Nature Communications* paper found that <cite index="136-1">the FAD-superoxide radical pair within cryptochrome can respond to Earth-strength magnetic fields provided the recombination reaction is strongly asymmetric, a scenario invoking the quantum Zeno effect</cite>, overturning the assumption that <cite index="136-1">closely bound pairs were unresponsive to weak fields due to arrested spin dynamics.</cite> Her lab also demonstrated the effect in living cells: magnetic-field-dependent fluorescence in engineered flavoprotein in live *E. coli*, with the characteristic non-monotonic dose-response that a radical-pair mechanism predicts.

**The elegant convergence, and the reason this belongs in your model.** Cryptochrome is the same protein family that functions as a circadian photoreceptor. The molecule at the centre of the magnetoreception literature is the molecule at the centre of the light-entrainment literature. And the radical partner in Aiello's work is **superoxide**, a reactive oxygen species, which is your Drain subsystem.

Light, magnetism and oxidative stress meet in one protein. That is a genuine synthesis point and it is published.

**Aiello's own methodological warning, which you should adopt verbatim.** Her group notes that <cite index="137-1">evidence of the biological impacts of weak magnetic fields has been reported for more than fifty years, but research progress has been hampered by a lack of systematics in most experiments</cite>, which is why her lab built automated, water-cooled Helmholtz coils to eliminate temperature confounds. Fifty years of suggestive, poorly-controlled results is exactly the state of the field. That is the correct amount of confidence to have.

## Scrutton and enzyme tunneling: real, and not actionable

Nigel Scrutton's work established that quantum tunneling occurs in enzyme-catalysed hydrogen transfer. Kinetic isotope effects exceeding the semiclassical limit are the evidence, and that part is accepted.

The contested part is whether protein "promoting vibrations" actively enhance tunneling rates. Arieh Warshel, Nobel laureate in chemistry, has published sustained critiques (Kamerlin, Mavri & Warshel, *FEBS Letters* 2010; Warshel & Bora, *J Chem Phys* 2016). Scrutton's own group concedes the point honestly: <cite index="147-1">other experimental evidence for tunnelling, and the potential role of promoting vibrations that transiently compress the reaction barrier, is more indirect</cite>, and elsewhere that <cite index="151-1">coupling of fast protein dynamics to enzyme chemistry is controversial and has ignited considerable debate, especially over the past 15 years, H-transfer can occur by quantum tunneling, and the temperature dependence of kinetic isotope effects has emerged as the gold standard descriptor, yet crucially, direct evidence for coupled motions is lacking.</cite>

**For your purposes:** every enzyme in your metabolic panel is already using quantum tunneling. It's a fact about how chemistry works, not a variable you can move. Nothing in your protocol changes it. Interesting; not a lever.

## What this adds to the model

Your stack from Addendum II gains a layer underneath everything:

| Layer | Mechanism | Timescale | Measurable in your program? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Layer 0, quantum substrate** | Tunneling in enzymes; vibronic energy transfer; tryptophan superradiance; radical-pair spin chemistry | fs-ps | **No** |
| Layer 1, the battery | Proton gradient, ~30 MV/m | ms | Indirectly |
| Layer 2, the plant | mtDNA capacity | lifetime | Not yet |
| Layer 3, the membrane | Cardiolipin, DHA | months | **Omega-3 Index** |
| Layer 4, the input | Light entrainment, DHA substrate | daily | **Vitamin D, logged light** |
| Layer 5, output & drain | Hormones, inflammation | 90 days | **The panel** |

**Layer 0 is real and it is not yours to move.** That's the honest bottom line, and stating it plainly is what will distinguish you from everyone else who invokes quantum biology.

The gap is timescale. Quantum effects in biology operate at femtoseconds to picoseconds. Your interventions operate at days to months. Fifteen orders of magnitude separate them. Nobody in this literature, not Kurian, not Aiello, not Scholes, has demonstrated a lifestyle intervention that measurably alters a quantum biological process in a human. **No marker on your panel changes because of anything in this addendum.**

**What it does give you is a bottom to the model.** When someone asks *why* DHA matters, or *why* light exposure matters, or *why* reactive oxygen species are worth measuring, you can now answer down to physics instead of stopping at correlation. That's a better explanation, not a new intervention, and the difference between those two things is exactly what a reviewer will test you on.

## Two testable hypotheses this generates

Worth writing down now, because they're the kind of thing that makes a research program look like a research program.

**H1, Tryptophan and oxidative load.** If Kurian's photoprotection mechanism holds, tryptophan status should interact with markers of oxidative and inflammatory load. Tryptophan is dietary, measurable, and already tracked in your food list. The kynurenine:tryptophan ratio is a validated, commercially available marker of inflammation-driven tryptophan degradation. Adding it to the Drain subsystem is cheap, is defensible on conventional immunology grounds alone, and would be the only marker on any longevity panel with a plausible quantum-optical rationale behind it.

**H2, Cryptochrome as the common node.** Circadian entrainment quality and oxidative status both route through flavin-containing photoreceptors. If they're mechanistically coupled, your logged light-exposure data and your hs-CRP deltas should co-vary more tightly than either does with adherence generally. You will have the data to look. Nobody else does.

Neither is provable in cohort 01. Both are worth stating in the protocol as pre-specified exploratory hypotheses, because a pre-registered hypothesis that doesn't pan out is science, and the same observation made afterward is a story.

## Revised reading order

Your ordering was good. One change and one addition:

1. **Al-Khalili & McFadden: *Life on the Edge*** (2014). Read first, not fifth. It's the calibration instrument. It'll show you the field's actual claims are narrower and stranger than the wellness usage, and it predates the FMO walk-back, so read it alongside point 2.
2. **Cao et al., "Quantum biology revisited," *Science Advances* 2020.** Free, and the necessary corrective to any popular account. Read immediately after Al-Khalili.
3. **Kurian: the 2024 *J Phys Chem B* superradiance paper**, plus his 2025 *Science Advances* single-author paper on the computational capacity of life. Most relevant to you.
4. **Aiello**: the 2024 *Nature Communications* cryptochrome paper and her lab's live-cell work.
5. **Ritz**: the 2000 *Biophysical Journal* foundational magnetoreception paper.
6. **Scholes**: the microtubule energy-diffusion work, which converges with Kurian.
7. **Scrutton**: one review. Enough to know tunneling is real and not a lever.
8. **Engel**: the 2007 paper, read as history, with the walk-back already in hand.

Olaya-Castro and Vedral are theorists whose work you'd read for depth on light-harvesting dynamics and quantum information in biology respectively. Neither is load-bearing for your model.
